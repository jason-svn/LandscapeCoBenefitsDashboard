Growth curves

Each benefit as a % of its own peak, so all five share one scale
Growth years: each stop on the Growth year slider models the same planted layout at a different tree age — 0 yr is the trees as planted (Growth Projections' Baseline workset), and each later year (5/10/15/20/25 by default, or any custom year up to 100) is its own growth-year workset (older models used one design option per year). They're snapshots of one site in time, not alternatives to add together, so figures are shown for one selected year at a time rather than summed — a "25 years" total blended with a "5 years" total would be meaningless. A tree stops growing once it reaches its type's mature size — the LDS max height/width the app downloads from Airtable — or 25 years after planting when no mature size is known, so later years level off; the growth curves shade the years after every tree has reached full size, where the figures stop changing. The curves show each benefit as a % of its own peak across the years, so litres, kilograms and money share one scale; the tooltip and the table give the real figures. When the model also has real design options, the Scenario dropdown picks one and the slider steps through that option's years.

Embodied carbon: the planting areas' one-off GWP — the product + transport stages of their materials from the landscape data sheet, as Floor Calculator records it. Vegetation's negative sheet "GWP" is its yearly sequestration, so it never counts as embodied carbon. Payback = embodied carbon ÷ the selected growth year's yearly CO₂e uptake (trees' CO₂ equivalent plus floors' sequestered carbon × 3.67), the same figure the Dashboard app shows.

How the "equivalent to" figures are calculated: water → baths at 150 L/bath; CO₂ → miles driven at 0.404 kg CO₂/mile (EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator, average passenger vehicle); oxygen → days of one person's breathing at ~0.84 kg O₂/day, with tree-sourced oxygen estimated from CO₂ uptake at a 32:44 mass ratio (O₂ released per CO₂ fixed); air pollutants and cost-saved equivalencies (cigarettes, cups of coffee) are illustrative round figures meant for public-facing communication, not regulatory claims — adjust EQUIV in this page's script to your organization's approved source. All mass/volume figures are Metric (kg, m³) regardless of the unit system the export was made under.
